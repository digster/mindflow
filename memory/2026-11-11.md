# 2026-11-11 — Quick-switch numbers beside the workspace switcher

Branch `ccr-9a13422b-3ncu2b`, on top of b7b921c (PR #15, workspaces, merged).
**UI only. No format, schema or migration change.**

## The ask

> when there is more than one workspace present, add numeric icons beside the
> workspace dropdown for quick switching.

## What was built

- **`src/ui/workspaceNumbers.ts`** (new). A `WorkspaceNumbers` strip of
  keycap-like buttons, `Workspace ▾ 1 [2] 3`, right after the switcher.
  - Shown only with two or more workspaces. With nine or fewer, one per
    workspace, in list order. Past nine the list behind the switcher has the
    rest (`MAX_QUICK_SWITCH`).
  - The workspace on screen is in the accent (`.is-current`, `aria-current`).
    Pressing it does nothing.
  - Tooltip: the name (`Sketches (current)` on the active one). Accessible name
    `2: Sketches`, digit first, so the visible label is in the name (voice
    control).
  - Buttons are pooled and reused, never rebuilt, so focus stays on the number
    just pressed.
  - `sync` compares `state.workspaces` by reference (the store replaces the
    array on every list change), so pans at 60 fps cost nothing.
  - Pure helpers for the unit tests: `quickSwitchTargets`, `numbersThatFit`,
    `quickSwitchLabel`.
- **Fitting.** The numbers are fixed-size, so they never shrink. They drop
  from the end. Every fit starts from all of them shown, reads the left
  group's overflow (`scrollWidth - clientWidth`), and takes away
  `ceil(overflow / step)`. A single number left becomes none.
  - A `ResizeObserver` on the **whole bar** refits on resize. Observing the
    group would not bring numbers back when the window widens (LEARNINGS).
  - Order of giving way: Edited label, board name (to 64px), numbers, then the
    switcher's own name. A `:has()` rule keeps the switcher rigid while any
    number shows. `:has()` is within the build targets (Chrome 111,
    Firefox 121, Safari 16.4).
  - Measured with default names: 3 numbers fit from about 953px, 9 from about
    1115px. Between 761 and 860px (compact switcher) 2 or 3 fit at the top of
    the band and none at the bottom. Below 760px the bar scrolls and all show.
  - On `pointer: coarse` the buttons are 32px, like the bar's other buttons.
- **Wiring:** `ToolbarCallbacks.onSwitchWorkspace`, which goes to
  `App.switchWorkspace`, so `leaveStage` runs as for the list and Page Down.

## Tests

- **Unit**, `workspaceNumbers.test.ts` (8): targets, the cap at nine, the fit
  arithmetic, the label. 786 in total.
- **E2E**, `workspaces.spec.ts` (+8):
  - They appear with a second workspace.
  - A press switches, keeps focus, and is not an edit.
  - An open text box keeps its text.
  - They follow a move, a rename and a delete.
  - They stop at nine.
  - They drop at 1024px with the switcher's name whole, and come back at 1280px.
  - All of them can be tapped at 390px.
  - Nothing is covered at 770 to 1000px.
  - `blockedControls` now skips controls that are not drawn.
- Full e2e **308 of 308**. Typecheck clean. `index.html` rebuilt.
- **Mutations**, each one reverted, each failing at least one test:
  - no fit
  - no rigid switcher
  - no `[hidden]` rule
  - no resize observer
  - buttons rebuilt on every sync (loses focus)
- Looked at it in Chromium: 1280 light and dark, 1024 with nine, 860, 390,
  an iPad Pro 11 in landscape (32px targets), and a keyboard focus ring.
  Screenshots deleted.

  `ResizeObserver` are standard in the build targets.
- The e2e suite ran through a scratch config pointing Playwright at
  `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`, as last session. The repo config is unchanged
  and the scratch config was deleted.

## Choices made without asking

- **Nine at most.** One digit per button.
- **Names over numbers.** The numbers give way before the workspace's name.
  The alternative was to switch to the compact switcher and keep the numbers.
  The highlighted number says where you are, but you lose the name at a
  glance.
- **No keyboard shortcut for the numbers.** None was asked for. `Alt+1..9`
  would be a natural follow-up.
